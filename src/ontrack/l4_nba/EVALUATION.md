## 1. Models (point-in-time, issue #3)

Training rows: cases **open and unresolved** at each monthly as_of 2025-11-01, 2025-12-01, 2026-01-01, 2026-02-01, 2026-03-01, 2026-04-01, 2026-05-01; early stopping and calibration on 2026-06-01; **test on 2026-07-01** (never seen in training). Labels are measured strictly after as_of: self-cure = cured within (as_of, as_of + 30 days]; payment = a payment through any contact in [as_of, as_of + 7 days).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- The old numbers (AUC 0.964 / 0.997) were leakage: rows included cases already cured at as_of, the payment label sat before the feature date, and features read 28-Sep values (current DPD, latest bureau pull, `accounts_in_case_count`, which is above 1 only for cases still open today).
- Sanity check: dropping the strongest feature does not collapse the AUC, so no single column carries the label.
- 'Cases resolved by 28 Sep' removes the cases still open today: on those the self-cure model is much weaker (about 0.58). Much of its skill is spotting cases that get stuck, which is what the ranker needs (who will not pay on their own), but it is not a precise clock for when a case cures.
- `payment_7d` is weak (payments are rare, 3%); it is only used as a 0.5x-2x responsiveness multiplier.

Top drivers of the self-cure model (mean |SHAP| on the test month): `bureau_delinquent_trades`, `inbound_30d`, `bureau_inquiries_6m`, `case_age_days`, `salary_drop_pct`, `hardship_mentioned_any`.

The order is right (observed self-cure rises steadily from decile 1 to 10), but the calibrator fitted on 2026-06-01 over-predicts in every decile: by up to 8 points, 4.6 on average. That is the base-rate drift in this data (cases open on 2026-06-01 self-cured 32% of the time within 30 days, on 2026-07-01 23%, on 2026-08-29 10%). It is why the agent card's probabilities come from a calibrator fitted on 2026-08-29, the latest month with a complete outcome and the closest to today's open book, and why the ranker uses a relative threshold (3.5x the open book's rate) rather than a fixed one. Ranking quality (AUC) does not depend on calibration.

### Payment within 7 days: what we tried to improve it (K12)

| experiment (same months, same split) | AUC valid | AUC test | vs current (test) |
|---|---|---|---|
| current model | 0.6059 | 0.5831 | 0.0000 |
| + response history (share of past outbound contacts followed by a payment in 7 days) | 0.6065 | 0.5765 | -0.0066 |
| + link clicks and recency | 0.6036 | 0.5819 | -0.0012 |
| + payday timing (days to the usual salary day) | 0.6030 | 0.5730 | -0.0101 |
| + all new features | 0.6077 | 0.5868 | 0.0037 |
| smaller trees (7 leaves, 400 cases per leaf) | 0.6075 | 0.5759 | -0.0072 |
| only cases contacted in the window (will they pay if contacted?) | 0.5957 | 0.5955 | 0.0124 |

Rule: replace the model only if test AUC improves by 0.03 or more, with K1 repeatability intact. No experiment came close, so the model stays as it is (`python -m ontrack.l4_nba.payment_experiments` reproduces the table). The reason is the label, not the features: in this data a payment is only recorded through a contact, and on 2026-07-01 36% of the open cases had no contact at all in the next 7 days:

| window | cases | payment observed |
|---|---|---|
| no contact in the 7 days | 11,678 | 0.0000 |
| at least one contact | 20,625 | 0.0479 |

For those cases the label is 0 whatever the customer would have done, and when the bank contacts someone is the bank's choice, not something customer features can predict. The model is therefore used only as a 0.5x-2x responsiveness multiplier in the ranker, never as a decision on its own. The conditional question (will they pay if contacted?) is the right target for a next version, once contacts are randomised (champion / challenger cells) so it can be learned without selection bias.

## 2b. Business value in dollars (conservative)

Same open book, same day: what OnTrack recommends against the bank's last recommendation (`collections_cases.nba_recommendation_last`), valued with measured numbers only.

| lever | bank's last recommendation | OnTrack | difference | unit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Expected payments within 7 days from the recommended contact (automated cases) | $124,523 (1,737 payments) | $281,070 (3,921 payments) | +$156,548 | per round of contacts |
| 2. Contact spend on customers who paid on their own anyway (the 20% each would leave alone) | $4,947 (4,911 contacts) found | $6,944 (6,225 contacts) found | +$1,997 | per month (2026-07-01 back-test) |
| 3. Calls that fit the day's call capacity | 34,326 calls (6.8 days of capacity) | 5,037 calls (1 day) | 29,289 fewer calls | capacity 5,037 calls a day |

**Method and assumptions** (each one chosen to understate OnTrack's value):
- Lever 1 values every expected payment at the **median recorded payment, $71.68** (the mean is about 2.6x higher), not at the overdue amount.
- The chance of a payment within 7 days of a contact is the **measured** rate per channel from `contact_history` (last 12 months), times the customer's responsiveness from the payment model (0.5x-2x), the same numbers the ranker uses. It is observed, not a randomised uplift.
- Only the 36,058 **automated** cases count. Nothing is claimed for the cases sent to a person (hardship, vulnerability, disputes, complaints, holds), where OnTrack deliberately does not collect.
- A bank recommendation the policy gate would block (no consent, holds, contact cap) counts as no payment: 10,650 of the bank's recommendations for automated cases could not legally be sent.
- Lever 2 uses what the bank actually spent (`contact_cost_cad`) in the 30 days after 2026-07-01 on cases that then cured on their own: OnTrack's leave-alone 20% contains 3,135 such cases, the bank model's 2,685. Leaving a case alone is not free: the rest of each 20% did not cure, which is why production waits only on cases above 3.5x the book's self-cure rate.
- Lever 3 is capacity, not dollars: the bank's engine recommends more calls than the team can make.
- Nothing is annualised: a round is one recommended contact per case.

## 6. Notes

- The 96,000 open cases were all opened Apr-Sep 2026 and never resolve inside the release, so later training months have lower self-cure rates (61% of cases open on 2026-04-01 cured within 30 days, 10% of those open on 2026-08-29). Production probabilities are calibrated on 2026-08-29, the latest month with a complete 30-day outcome; test metrics use the June calibrator.
- The bank's no-contact holdout cured as often as its champion strategy (63.4% vs 62.7% within 30 days). The holdout still had some contacts, so this is a hint, not proof, that contact adds little for customers who would pay anyway; it is why OnTrack waits on likely self-cures.
- Training is repeatable: fixed seeds, deterministic LightGBM on 4 threads, rows read in a fixed order and inputs rounded to 6 decimals, so two runs give the same AUCs and the same 96,000 recommendations.
